## Matchup analysis (fighter terms)

**Range and striking.** Duncan has 4 inches of reach, a switch stance, and a high-volume, accurate, hard-to-hit profile (4.45 landed vs 2.36 absorbed per minute, 54-58% accuracy). Allen's defense is leaky (47-48%, 3.78 absorbed/min) and he has been hurt by power before (Curtis 2021 TKO; rocked again in 2024; lost to the technical strikers Imavov and Hernandez on volume). On the feet Duncan should win most exchanges and has finishing power (10 KO wins). Allen's Chicago/Valle camp is a striking-focused setup, so expect better boxing and pressure than in the Kill Cliff days, but not a range win.

**Wrestling vs takedown defense.** This is the fight. Duncan's TDD is only about 57-63%, he was taken down 5 times by Cannonier (but lost only 6:12 control time, much against the cage), held down by Dolidze and dominated by Rodrigues (4 TDs, 8:20 control, the only loss in the streak). Allen shoots 1.5 per 15 minutes at about 41%, so he is not a high-volume wrestler, but he gets takedowns in spurts (he took Imavov down in R1) and from the clinch/cage. Duncan has virtually no offensive wrestling, so he will not take Allen down; the fight stays standing only if Duncan's range keeps Allen from closing.

**Grappling danger.** Allen is a BJJ black belt with 7 UFC submission wins, most recently finishing Craig in R3. Duncan has not been reported submitted, but his top-control exposure against high-level grapplers (Rodrigues, Dolidze) is exactly the scenario that matters. A single clean takedown into back-take per round is Allen's fastest route. Duncan's athleticism (basketball, long limbs) helps scrambles and getting up.

**Cardio over 5 rounds.** Allen: proven (Curtis 25 min, de Ridder faded first). He warned he wants to take Duncan into "deep water." Duncan: no cardio flags in 3-rounders but untested past R3 (**unverified**); his late-round output is a real unknown, and Allen's pace plus body/clinch grind is built for it.

**Likely game plans.** Allen (Valle): jab/cross to enter, check low kicks, level-change off every Duncan exchange, work the clinch to the cage, hunt back-takes; accept some strikes early to get inside. Duncan (Range): stay on the end of his jab/long front kicks, switch stances, circle off the cage, sprawl and hand-fight to avoid control, use knees/flying knees (his CW 136 finish) and power counters, force Allen to chase for 5 rounds.

**Paths to victory.**
- Allen: (1) takedown-to-submission in rounds 1-3 (about 25% per Kalshi); (2) accumulate cage control and top time across 5 rounds for a decision (27%); (3) survive Duncan's early power and take over late as in the de Ridder fight.
- Duncan: (1) KO/TKO from range or a counter as Allen shoots (28-30%); (2) high-volume striking decision while sprawling most shots (his Cannonier template: big strike edge, survive cage control); (3) late stoppage if Allen's defensive lapses and 3.78 absorbed/min add up.

**Lean.** Near pick'em. If forced, I lean Duncan by a hair (roughly 52-55%) on striking differential, reach, form (5 straight, 84-day turnaround) and the models (52-63% Duncan), while the market (earlier 54% Allen) and the Kalshi snapshot favor Allen. Low confidence: Duncan's upside rests on his takedown defense holding against a submission specialist, which has been inconsistent, and on 5-round cardio that has not been tested. Duncan as the (slight) underdog at 1.97 has a very real path - he is arguably the stats favorite. The cleanest read: if Allen scores early takedowns (R1-R2), Allen is the clear favorite; if the first two rounds stay standing at range, Duncan is. The app's 1.86 on Allen is better value than the market was showing; Duncan 1.97 is slightly worse than the market was showing.
