## Matchup analysis (my judgment, built on the facts above)

**Range and style.** Both are roughly the same size: Wellmaker 5'10" / 71" reach, switch; Tanzilovi 5'9" / 70", orthodox. No range edge. Wellmaker is the harder, more explosive puncher. Tanzilovi is the more accurate (68% vs 55%) but low-output-damage kickboxer who has not finished anyone above regional level.

**Power and durability.** Wellmaker has the only proven fight-ender in the pairing (three straight R1 KOs under 2:40, plus the late-R1 knockdown he suffered and the Díaz flash knockdown suggest he is also hittable). Tanzilovi has never been stopped (both losses by decision) but his best competition is Collins, Musasa and Torres; he has not eaten power like Wellmaker's on this level. Chin quality is unknown.

**Wrestling vs takedown defense.** This is the fight. Wellmaker's takedown defense is 46-47%, he has been taken down 8 of 13 times in his last two fights, and both losses were wrestling-driven. Tanzilovi's single UFC outing showed 4.5 TD per 15 and control time (about 4:14 per one snippet). Opposite side: Wellmaker's own takedowns are 0.00 per 15, so he is not a wrestling threat and relies entirely on the stand-up exchange. Tanzilovi's TD accuracy figures conflict (36% vs 5/9), so his shot-finishing against a real wrestler is unproven. Wellmaker did not get dominated on the feet, though: 77/146 with Ewing, so he is only a problem when the fight gets taken to the mat.

**Cardio.** Wellmaker's three UFC wins ended inside 3 minutes, so he has not been tested late; his losses went to R2 (sub) and R3 (decision). Tanzilovi is fighting back down at 135 after a featherweight debut and has been inactive since June; weight cut effect unknown. Neither shows a cardio blowup in the data. Wellmaker's late-fight pace is a real question after the Ewing fight (decision loss, 30-27 on one card).

**Coach and game plan.** Wellmaker's switch to ATT is a direct answer to wrestling holes, but it has been about 20 weeks; whether the takedown defense is better is unproven. Tanzilovi's coaches: not found. The obvious plan for Tanzilovi is an early scare-the-power respect game, then single legs and control for 15 minutes; Wellmaker's obvious plan is to hit the first big counter before the fight is taken to the mat.

**Paths.**
- Wellmaker: R1 right-hand KO (priced +290 in R1); or a clean ATT-improved sprawl-and-brawl decision.
- Tanzilovi: takedown-heavy decision (as Ewing and Díaz did), or he survives R1 and grinds out control/ground-and-pound or a back-take; submission is a stretch, as no sub history was found.

**Lean.** Slight lean Tanzilovi at +183 as a value play, low confidence. Reasoning: market no-vig is about 66% Wellmaker, but independent models run 49.8-53% (UFC Calendar, Combat Edge), the only outlier on Wellmaker's side being ATS.io (73%). Wellmaker's last two losses came precisely to a wrestling-first opponent, and Tanzilovi's stat profile is the grappling-leaning one. My rough fair price is about Wellmaker -130 / Tanzilovi +110. The downside: Wellmaker's R1 KO threat is real (+290 R1 is not far off) and the camp change may have fixed the very hole being priced. Neither side is a strong play; if betting, Tanzilovi/over or Tanzilovi by decision is more in line with the matchup than a straight Wellmaker KO, but check lines after weigh-ins. Does the underdog have a real path? Yes: takedowns plus 15 minutes of control is a proven recipe against Wellmaker. He is a live dog, not a long shot.
