## Matchup analysis

Range and striking
- Fili is 4 in taller with a 5 in reach edge (74" vs 69"). His best version uses that length: jab, front/lead-leg kicks, keeping Kamaka at the end of his punches.
- Kamaka is the more accurate, higher-output striker on paper (59% vs 37%, about 5.5 vs 3.9 sig strikes/min). He needs to close distance and hit combinations at pocket range. Statistically he also gets hit more (about 5.7-6.0 absorbed/min vs Fili's 4.2-4.6), so volume exchanges are not free for him.
- Fili's accuracy is poor (35-37%), but his recent fights show he can throw volume (e.g., 62/141 vs Delgado, 64/182 vs Rodriguez). Volume is not the same as damage; the question is whether judges see more of his or Kamaka's.

Wrestling vs takedown defense
- Fili: about 2.2 TD/15 at 45% accuracy; he attempted 6 and 5 in his last two completed fights, so he is not shy about shooting. He defends 71-72%.
- Kamaka: wrestling base, about 1.4-1.5 TD/15 at 54%, but only 55% TDD, which is his clearest soft spot against a BJJ-based fighter. This is the main reason the Combat Edge model liked the "takedown accuracy" angle (against Kamaka).
- Net: grappling is Fili's most reliable route to a scorecard edge and to a possible submission (0.2 sub att/15, so low). Kamaka's wrestling credentials mean he can scramble and avoid being held down, but the numbers favor Fili in this phase. If Kamaka stuffs the first few shots, Fili's path narrows.

Durability and finishing
- Both have recent stoppage losses: Kamaka R1 TKO to Riley (controversially early), Fili R2 TKO to Oliveira and R1 guillotine to Costa. Neither is a high-percentage finisher: Kamaka's wins are mostly decisions; Fili's finish rate is not found.
- Fili is 36 with several late-career stoppage losses; Kamaka's chin question rests on two stoppage losses (2020 and 2026). The market prices Fili +225 to KO and Kamaka +525, reflecting size and power expectations rather than recent results.

Cardio and pace
- Kamaka's decision-heavy record and short-notice 15-minute outing vs Hope in April suggest sound conditioning. Fili has a long history of 15-minute fights; at 36 his late-round output is a question mark but I found no source that says he fades (unverified).
- 112 vs 91 days since last fights: neither is rushed or ring-rusty.

Coach game plans
- Not found for either side. Reasonable inference only: Kamaka said "our tactics are way different" and that he will stick to his style (his preview quote), consistent with pressure and volume; Fili's style historically is distance striking plus takedown mixes (inference, unverified).

Paths to victory
- Fili: use reach and kicks early, mix in takedowns to bank rounds and to avoid Kamaka's pocket volume, win a decision (market's most likely single outcome), or catch a tiring/over-eager Kamaka with a counter or guillotine/back-take. Danger: Kamaka's volume/accuracy in the pocket and Fili's recent habit of absorbing damage.
- Kamaka: stay on the inside of Fili's kicks, land 5-6 clean strikes per minute and win rounds by volume, defend or scramble out of the first takedown attempts, and avoid Riley-style fence exchanges. A stoppage is plausible only if Fili's durability issues show again.

## Lean
- Straight-up lean: Fili, narrowly (roughly 52-55%), most likely by decision. Reasons: reach/size, better takedown defense and takedown volume than Kamaka can match, 25+ fight UFC experience, and Kamaka's recent early stoppage loss and soft 55% TDD.
- Reservation: Fili is 36, has lost 4 of his last 5 (3 by stoppage/sub, one close split), and the market (about 57%) is a bit rich for that form. Kamaka's output and accuracy are real advantages.
- Does the underdog have a real path? Yes. Kamaka at +115 to +138 is a live dog; his path is volume plus accuracy over 15 minutes and good takedown defense. If you want value rather than the straight-up winner, Kamaka +120 or better is the better price. Confidence overall is low; treat as close to a coin flip.
